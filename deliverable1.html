Deliverable 1: Is the “Martingale” strategy profitable?¶

Explanation of Roulette and the Martingale strategy¶

First off, let's talk about roulette. It is a gambling game, where a player can bet money to see where a ball will land on a spinning wheel. Once the wheel slows, the ball has three outcomes: land in red, black, or green. There are 18 red spots, 18 black, and 2 green.

Let's say that a person bets black, and the ball lands on black, when they bet $2, they gain $4 total (2 dollars in profit!). If someone bets red, and bet $2, they lose their $2. If ball lands on green, all players lose money, unless they bet on green.

In the long run, the house gains money from this game, and players lose money. But what if there was a strategy that could allow players to make money in the long run? Let's look at a possible one.

The Martingale strategy essentially has a player pick either red or black. They will only choose one color to bet on for the rest of the trials. If a person bets on red, and the ball lands on red, they gain $1.

However, let's say a player bets $1 on red, and the ball lands on black. The player then doubles up the bet, betting $2. If they lose that, they bet $4, and it lands on red. This outcome leads the gambler to be up a dollar. Then they will bet $1 and so on. No matter how long the losing streak is, this strategy seemingly allows the next win to get your money back plus a dollar. Hence, why it seems like a good strategy.

Let's take a look at a simulation, and see if this makes money over time.

Simulation¶

Essentially, the code below will have the computer run a bunch of simulations, not actually playing real roulette, the computer randomly chooses whether black, red, or green is picked.

For clarity:

  • B stands for player's budget, or how much money they have at the start, that being 200 dollars.
  • W stands for the winning goal, that being 300 dollars.
  • L is the time limit, specifically 200 spins.
  • M is the casino's maximum bet, or the highest amount of money a player is allowed to bet on one spin, which is 100 dollars.

The player keeps playing until they either hit their 300 dollar goal, hit 200 spins, or go broke.

import numpy as np
from itertools import groupby
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import pandas as pd

def oneplay(B=200, W=300, L=200, M=100, summary = True):

    # martingale
    def wager(ledger):
        if ledger["outcome"][-1] == 1:
            new_wager = 1
        else:
            new_wager = min(2*ledger["wager"][-1], M, ledger["budget"][-1])
        ledger["wager"].append(new_wager)

    def spin(ledger):
        spin = np.random.binomial(1, p = 18/38)
        ledger["outcome"].append(spin)

    def stop_rule(ledger):
        if ledger["outcome"][-1] == 1:
            ledger["budget"].append(ledger["budget"][-1] + ledger["wager"][-1])
        else:
            ledger["budget"].append(ledger["budget"][-1] - ledger["wager"][-1])
        
        if ledger["budget"][-1] >= W:
            outcome = 1
        elif len(ledger["budget"]) - 1 >= L:
            outcome = -1
        elif ledger["budget"][-1] <= 0:
            outcome = -2
        else:
            outcome = 0
        return outcome

    # ledger
    ledger = {"budget":[B, B], "wager":[0], "outcome":[1]}
    outcome = 0
    while outcome == 0:
        wager(ledger)
        spin(ledger)
        outcome = stop_rule(ledger)

    if summary == False:
        return ledger

    bad_luck = max([len(list(values)) for key, values in groupby(ledger["outcome"]) if key == 0])
        
    summary = {
          "B":B
        , "W":W
        , "L":L
        , "M":M
        , "outcome":outcome
        , "profit":ledger["budget"][-1] - B
        , "n_games":len(ledger["budget"]) - 1
        , "prop_red":np.mean(ledger["outcome"])
        , "bad_luck_streak": bad_luck
    }

    return summary

Here's essentially what's happening in the above code cell.

  • wager decides how much to bet. After a win, the computer bets $1. After a loss, it doubles the last bet, but never more than the casino's max or the money the player has left.
  • spin is the roulette wheel. The player wins with a probability of 18/38.
  • stop_rule adds or subtracts the bet from the budget. The computer then checks whether the player should stop: they reached $300, went broke, or hit 200 spins.
  • The ledger essentially records every bet, every result, and the budget after each spin.

The function oneplay runs one full trip to the casino. With summary=False, it gives back the whole ledger so we can plot it.

The cell above shows the average profit in over 10,000 simulated trips to the casino. A player on average, loses about 36 dollars, indicating that using the Martingale betting strategy loses money.

This answers the question, that no, the Martingale strategy is not profitable. But what if there was no time limit? What if gamblers were able to stay at the casino forever at the roulette wheel, without having to eat, drink, sleep? Would they still lose money or eventually reach the profit goal?

time_limits = [50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 10000]
avg_profit = []

for L in time_limits:
    trips = pd.DataFrame([oneplay(L=L) for i in range(5000)])
    avg_profit.append(trips.profit.mean())

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 5))

This chart shows that even an extreme amount of time can't save the gambler. It makes things worse, and they lose even more money with the Martingale strategy. With 50 spins, a gambler can expect to lose about 10 dollars. However, with 10000 spins, they can expect to lose about 50 dollars.

Conclusion¶

Do NOT use the Martingale strategy if your goal is to make money. While you might make small bits of money here and there, a losing streak either often leads to reaching the casino bet limit, limiting the ability to get a profit, or just lose it all. This is true for most if not all strategies. Every spin is in favor of the house, so no strategy in roulette can meaningfully change that.

10,000 simulations show that the gambler will lose an average value of about 36 dollars. Increasing the amount of time the gambler has doesn't even help either, it makes things worse.
